JUDGE · CHI 2027 Workshop

Judgments, Uncertainties, and Decisions in Generative AI Safety Practice

Making LLM-based systems safe rests on a stack of human judgments: where to draw the line, which cases to test, and what to assume when a policy is ambiguous. This workshop brings researchers and practitioners together to surface these judgments and explore AI safety as a human practice as much as a technical exercise.

About the Workshop

AI safety is often regarded as the most technical of the efforts around responsible AI, centered on questions about data and models. Yet when practitioners make LLM-based systems safer for their particular applications, whether by building guardrails themselves or plugging in a vendor's moderation service, they fill in ambiguous policies, unknown harms, and opaque models with their own assumptions. These judgments are shaped by organizational and cultural contexts, and they decide whose safety gets prioritized, yet the "technical" framing of safety leaves them largely unexamined.

Workshop Theme Human Judgments in AI Safety Practice

The assumptions, interpretations, and decisions that practitioners make under uncertainty when making LLM-based systems safer for their applications, shaped by their organizational and cultural contexts.

The workshop aims to:

  • Surface the human assumptions, interpretations, and decisions in safety practice, and explore how they shape safety workflows and outcomes across the safety pipeline.
  • Create dialogue between research and practice, sharing what worked and what did not, and building a shared vocabulary across HCI, RAI, and AI safety.
  • Explore how context shapes safety judgments, with particular attention to the Global South.
  • Map what transfers from HCI research on RAI practice to LLM safety, and where methods such as checklists, toolkits, and documentation need rethinking.
  • Co-create a research agenda and a community around AI safety in HCI.

Who Should Attend

We welcome researchers and practitioners from academia, industry, government, and non-profits working in HCI, Responsible AI, AI safety, or related fields, whether or not they see their work as "technical."

Researchers Those studying AI safety, responsible AI, or practitioner work in HCI, NLP, ML, and adjacent fields, including those reflecting on the judgments behind their own benchmarks, taxonomies, and evaluations.
Practitioners Data scientists, engineers, product and program managers, and others who make LLM-based systems safer in practice, from building guardrails and preference data to choosing and configuring off-the-shelf safeguards.
Governance & Policy People who write the policies, requirements, and standards that safety work tries to implement, and who are interested in how those get interpreted on the ground.

Workshop Activities

The workshop runs as two consecutive 90-minute sessions with a break in between: the first focuses on perspectives from research, and the second on perspectives from practice.

Session 1: Perspectives from Research

90 min
15 min

Welcome and "Safe Enough?" warm-up

Participants position themselves along a line in the room, from "safe enough to deploy" to "not safe enough," in response to borderline AI safety scenarios, showing how differently people draw the line.

30 min

Researcher keynote

An invited researcher reflects on the human judgments embedded in how AI safety research is conducted, from benchmarks and taxonomies to evaluation practices.

30 min

Lightning talks

Authors of accepted submissions give short talks (about 3 minutes each), grouped by related themes.

15 min

Author Q&A panel

Speakers come together for a moderated Q&A, with emerging themes captured on a shared board that feeds into Session 2.

Break

Session 2: Perspectives from Practice

90 min
30 min

Practitioner keynote

An invited AI safety practitioner shares how they made an LLM-based system safer in a particular setting: what they were uncertain about, where they drew the line, and what they would do differently.

35 min

Judgment tracing

In groups of 5–6, participants take up a real-world case and trace backward from the incident along the stages of a safety workflow to identify where judgments were made, by whom, under what uncertainty, and in which context. Groups then revisit the case from different roles to see how "safe enough" shifts depending on where one sits.

15 min

Group share-outs

Each group presents their traced case and key insights to the room.

10 min

Synthesis and next steps

Organizers draw together the judgments, open questions, and possible interventions into an initial research agenda, and outline plans for post-workshop outputs.

Call for Participation

We invite submissions of 1–4 pages (excluding references) in ACM single-column format that discuss specific human judgments in AI safety practice and the factors that shaped them, in one of two forms:

  • Paper-style contributions, such as position papers, preliminary findings, works in progress, or late-breaking work.
  • Ideas and reflections, such as provocations, accounts from AI safety practice, reflections on the judgments behind one's own published AI safety work (e.g., at NeurIPS or ACL) that did not make it into the paper, or reflections on how AI safety research is conducted more broadly.

Submissions will be reviewed by the organizers and invited reviewers from HCI, RAI, and AI safety, based on relevance to the theme and potential to spark discussion, while ensuring diversity of perspectives across academia and industry, disciplines, and regions. The judgments raised in accepted submissions will serve as starting material for the group activities.

With authors' consent, accepted papers will appear on CEUR-WS, with a non-archival option. At least one author per accepted submission must attend in person.
